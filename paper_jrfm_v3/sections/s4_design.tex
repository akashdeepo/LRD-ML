\section{Forecasting Design}\label{sec:design}

\subsection{Models}

All models are linear regressions of the target \eqref{eq:target} on
predictors dated $t$, estimated stock by stock. The baseline, model $A$, is
the HAR model of \citet{corsi2009simple} in logs with a return term,
\begin{equation}
y_{i,t,h} = \beta_0 + \beta_d \ln RV^{(1)}_{i,t} + \beta_w \ln RV^{(5)}_{i,t}
+ \beta_m \ln RV^{(22)}_{i,t} + \gamma_1 r_{i,t} + \gamma_2 \lvert r_{i,t}\rvert
+ \varepsilon_{i,t,h},
\label{eq:har}
\end{equation}
where $RV^{(k)}_{i,t}$ is the mean Parkinson variance over days $t-k+1$ to
$t$ and $r_{i,t}$ is the day-$t$ return. HAR-X, model $A_1$, adds the
levels of the VIX and MOVE indices at the close of day $t$. The persistence
blocks are added one at a time to HAR and then jointly, and three further
specifications, fixed before any result was computed, add a single block to
HAR-X so that the comparison with HAR-X isolates it.
Table~\ref{tab:models} lists the predictors.

\subsection{Estimation}

Each model is re-estimated by ordinary least squares at every weekly origin
on an expanding window of that stock's past rows. Because targets overlap for
$h>1$, a past row enters the regression only if its target window has closed
by the origin: row $j$ is used at origin $t$ only if day $j+h$ is no later
than day $t$. Without this restriction the last rows of each training sample
contain variance that is not yet observed, which at $h=22$ biases the
comparison in favour of richer models. The first 431 origins (to 14 June
2013) form the initial training sample, and all models are evaluated over
the same 646 origins from 17 June 2013 to 14 April 2026, on the same
(date, stock) cells.

\subsection{Evaluation}

The loss is the squared error of the log-variance forecast, pooled over
stocks and dates; Appendix~\ref{app:extra} reports QLIKE on variance
forecasts corrected point-in-time for the log transformation. Comparisons
rest on the cross-sectional mean loss differential per date,
$\bar\delta_t = N^{-1}\sum_i (L^{(1)}_{i,t} - L^{(2)}_{i,t})$, which absorbs
the strong cross-sectional correlation of forecast errors. We report three
tests on $\bar\delta_t$, each with a Newey--West variance and bandwidth
$\max(\lceil h/5\rceil - 1, \lfloor 4(T/100)^{2/9}\rfloor)$, which is six
weeks for $T=646$:

\begin{itemize}
\item the Diebold--Mariano test of equal mean loss
\citep{diebold1995comparing} with the small-sample correction of
\citet{harvey1997testing};
\item for linear models that nest HAR-X, the \citet{clark2007approximately}
test, which adjusts the loss differential for the noise introduced by
estimating parameters whose population value is zero, and so tests whether
the extra predictors have nonzero population coefficients;
\item the conditional predictive ability test of \citet{giacomini2006tests},
with the constant and the most recent observed differential
$\bar\delta_{t-\lceil h/5\rceil}$ as instruments, which asks whether the
estimated model, with its estimation error, forecasts better.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Registration}

The study was conducted in stages, and each stage was recorded in a dated
experiment ledger before its results were computed: the specification, the
comparison, the test and the decision rule. The rule for the registered
HAR-X extensions is that a specification adds information beyond HAR-X at a
horizon only if its Holm-adjusted \citep{holm1979simple} Clark--West $p$-value
is below 0.05 and its mean squared error is lower than HAR-X's. After the
results were known we added a requirement that the gain be at least 0.5\%;
we report it separately because it was not registered. A complete audit of
the pipeline preceded the final run; every correction was registered before
the corrected run and is listed in the ledger, which is public with the code
(Appendix~\ref{app:ledger}).
